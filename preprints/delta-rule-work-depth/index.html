Work–Depth Trade-offs for Exact Delta-Rule Computation

Tao Jiang* and Minbo Gao*

* Equal contribution (co-first authors).

First posted November 25, 2026

Read PDF BibTeX

Abstract

The delta rule updates a state st∈ℝd by st=st−1+βtkt(vt−kt⊤st−1) and is the memory update of DeltaNet and related linear-recurrent sequence models. Evaluated sequentially, it uses O(Nd) arithmetic operations on a sequence of length N but has depth linear in N. We study how much work exact arithmetic circuits need at a given depth. Assuming the recently claimed square and rectangular matrix multiplication exponents (in particular ω=9/4), we show that for d=N and every fixed 0<θ<1 the outputs can be computed in depth Nθ with work N2+(1−θ)2/4+o(1), and in polylogarithmic depth with work N9/4+o(1); unconditionally, work N2+o(1) is optimal at every depth N1+o(1). Conversely, exact dense triangular substitution reduces to the delta rule even with gates in (0,1), and through this reduction a recently claimed depth lower bound for iterated matrix products implies that every circuit needs depth Ω(logNloglogN), whatever its work. Finally, the arrays that a blockwise algorithm materializes have work exponents exactly matching our upper bounds, and a polynomial work lower bound at polylogarithmic depth would follow from a lower bound on an interior two-slice rank of iterated matrix multiplication.
