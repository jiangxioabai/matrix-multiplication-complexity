Exact Batched Top-1 Prediction Is as Hard as Matrix Multiplication

Tao Jiang and Minbo Gao

Co-first authors with equal contribution

Read PDF BibTeX

Abstract

Exact batched Top-1 prediction asks, given query vectors x1,…,xN and candidate vectors w1,…,wN in ℝd, for the least index maximizing ⟨xi,wj⟩ for every i; greedy decoding performs this operation at the output layer of a language model. Computing the full product XW⊤ by fast matrix multiplication and scanning its rows takes Nω+o(1) operations at d=N, but the output is only a list of indices, and the lower-bound techniques available for such discrete outputs do not reach beyond quadratic cost. We prove that in the real arithmetic model with exact comparisons and branching, every deterministic algorithm at d=N has an execution with at least half the border rank of ⌊N/2⌋×⌊N/2⌋ matrix multiplication in multiplications and divisions, so the complexity is Ω(Nω) and hence Nω+o(1). The proof rests on a general theorem that extracts a border decomposition of a bilinear map Q from the sign tests of any comparison tree deciding z≥Q(u,v). The lower bound persists with exponential and logarithm gates, on inputs with distinct scores, for Top-k outputs, and with free separate preprocessing of both matrices; at width d=Na we prove Ω(Ndω−1) against the upper bound Nω(1,a,1)+o(1), and show that N1+a+o(1) comparisons are necessary and sufficient.
