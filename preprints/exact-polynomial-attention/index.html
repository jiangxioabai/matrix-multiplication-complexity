Polynomial attention

The Arithmetic Complexity of Exact Polynomial Attention

Tao Jiang and Minbo Gao

Co-first authors with equal contribution

Read PDF BibTeX

Abstract

Polynomial attention replaces the exponential kernel of softmax attention by a fixed polynomial, such as the degree-r Taylor truncation of ex. We study the exact arithmetic complexity of normalized polynomial attention with N queries and keys of width d=Na, in the extended arithmetic circuit model with unrestricted exp and ln gates, where exact softmax attention has recently been shown to require Nω(1,a,1)±o(1) operations. We first show that, for every fixed even degree, exponential and logarithm gates, normalization, and correctness on only an open set of inputs can all be removed with constant overhead, which reduces the problem to the algebraic complexity of the unnormalized numerator. For quadratic attention we then determine the exponent exactly: it is ω(1,a,a). For higher degrees, polynomial selectors embed a direct sum of Nmin{(r−2)a,1−a} independent rectangular matrix products into a single attention call, which yields a degree-dependent lower bound. Under the recently claimed closed form for rectangular matrix multiplication exponents, quadratic attention has exponent 1+a+a2/4, every even degree r≥4 is strictly harder than quadratic attention whenever a<1, and degree r remains strictly easier than softmax attention whenever a<4(2−1)/r.
